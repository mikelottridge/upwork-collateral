What changed on the visual attention test?

This worked example starts with The TOVA Company’s published case of a 16-year-old who took the visual Test of Variables of Attention (T.O.V.A.) twice. These are the publisher’s example results. Mike’s contribution here is the source-linked summary and family-language drafts.

Two comparable sessions

The July 1, 2017 visual baseline lists no treatment entry. The September 1 visual session records medication taken two hours before testing (September summary, p. 17). An August auditory session used sound cues and is excluded from this visual-to-visual comparison.

What a clinician should check

The publisher records acceptable session and response validity on p. 2 and p. 17. The July notes specify that the performance-validity indicator applies from age 17; this learner was 16. Repeated exposure and different session conditions limit causal interpretation. These figures alone do not establish reliable change, treatment efficacy, or improvement at school.

Brief explanation for a family

In July, the learner missed 15 signals on the screen. In September, the learner responded to every target. There were still six presses when the learner was supposed to wait. What have you noticed about attention at home or school, if anything?

Spanish draft for bilingual review

En julio, hubo 15 señales en la pantalla que quedaron sin respuesta. En septiembre, respondió a todas las señales que requerían una respuesta. Aun así, pulsó el botón seis veces cuando debía esperar. ¿Qué han observado, si acaso, sobre la atención en casa o en la escuela?

What to bring to the next conversation

Ask the family or teacher for one concrete example from a familiar routine. Record what instructions were given, which steps the learner followed independently, and where a reminder helped. Daily observations add context that this computer task cannot provide.

Practitioner review is required before sharing. These sessions describe performance on the task; the scores alone cannot show why it changed. The publisher supplied the test results. The family wording and follow-up question are original. See quarter counts on p. 2 and p. 17.
Practice demonstration · Practitioner workflow · 3 of 3

What work could come off your desk?

The publisher already scores the test. The additional work in a practice is finding the comparable sessions, explaining which observations matter, and preparing language the family can use.

Start your review with the relevant records together

The first page places two dated results beside their source pages. You can check the modality, testing conditions, qualifier and original value before accepting any sentence. For example, the July omission count is 2 + 4 + 4 + 5; the September quarters each show zero.

Edit the explanation once

Use the source facts to review a short family explanation and its Spanish draft. Retain uncertainty when discussing medication or school performance. A qualified bilingual reviewer should check the selected language before clinical use.

Reserve the decision for the practitioner

Reconcile the test with history, interview and everyday observations. Approve, edit or withhold the interpretation and the proposed follow-up. The sample illustrates the output a custom workflow could prepare. It does not demonstrate a live T.O.V.A. integration.

Test whether the workflow earns its place

In a small pilot, compare usual preparation with assisted preparation on comparable authorized cases. Count correction and translation-review time in both. Check every score, date, qualifier and source link, and ask families to explain one change and one uncertainty in their own words. For an initial ten authorized cases, stop release on any source mismatch, lost qualifier or material translation error. Have the clinician approve every interpretation and a qualified bilingual reader verify translated copy. Continue only if total preparation and review time falls while families can explain the finding and its limits.
