# Assessment report demonstration

The original application used prior visits to analyze change, compared listening and visual performance in the most recent assessment, and paired the report with suggested training. This portfolio example uses three fictional referral contexts and invented task counts. Its count ranges summarize the invented activity scores. Each score counts correct responses in 20 opportunities.

The report offers 72 downloadable observations and 12 prepared AI-written passages. English and Spanish readers can choose a short, plain-language version or a more detailed explanation of the same findings. The presentation uses stored examples so a prospective client can explore them without sending data to a service.

Mike reports that drafting explanations of difficult concepts and translating them saved the practitioner considerable time. The portfolio uses this qualitative account. No measured duration or percentage was available.

The practitioner preview separates its controls from three client-facing pages: a family summary, assessment history, and current skills with a practice plan. The fictional client PDF illustrates case A. Printing the preview produces the selected case and family narrative. Dated fictional observations from September 2025 to March 2026 illustrate gains. Directly labeled bars compare listening and visual cues within a skill on a common 20-item scale. The fictional series illustrates progress tracking and makes no causal claim about therapy.


## Practitioner review and publisher comparison

The primary [worked example](../site/attention-source-review.pdf) starts with the [T.O.V.A. publisher case](https://files.tovatest.com/downloads/example-reports/16-year-old%20female,%20combined%20reports.pdf). The two visual sessions are comparable in modality. The intervening auditory assessment is excluded. Source checks used combined PDF pages 2, 3, 17, 25 and 26. The score floor is retained and the family draft distinguishes omissions from commissions. This example was checked against the source manually; no T.O.V.A. import or live clinical integration was tested.

BrainTrain's [IVA-2 Standard Report](https://www.braintrain.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/IVA-2-Standard-Report.pdf) supplies a useful comparison for validity, normative interpretation and examiner responsibility. MHS's [Conners CPT 3 overview](https://cdn.mhs.com/mhsdocs/Marketing_Files/ClinEd/Conners_CPT-3/CPT3_Brochure_0226_HUB.pdf) describes a 1,400-person normative sample and distinguishes response speed, variability, missed targets and incorrect responses. Neither publisher's scales should be transferred to the invented practice tasks.

Four local Claude Opus review passes challenged the baseline report, the first revision, the corrections, and the two final source checks. Codex checked the public publisher pages directly; Claude reviewed the supplied report text and source notes. Both accepted the revised work as a portfolio demonstration. A licensed practitioner has not validated it. A practice pilot would require authorization for the records, an appropriate data-handling workflow, clinical sign-off, and bilingual review. Measure total time including correction and translation review, check each numeric field and qualifier against its source, and ask a family to explain a finding and its uncertainty. Time savings in the original engagement were reported qualitatively; no measured savings are claimed for this example.
